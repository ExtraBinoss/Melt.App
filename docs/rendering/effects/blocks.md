# Blocks

Blocks rebuilds the source photograph as a mosaic of 2×1 plastic bricks. Each rectangular tile has two raised circular studs, a beveled perimeter, a dark stud sidewall, a directional cast shadow and a tight plastic highlight. It is a standalone source-image effect. The existing Gradient Blocks treatment continues to operate on its generated gradient.

The primary visual reference is the stud-based mosaic construction shown in the official [LEGO Art World Map booklet](https://www.lego.com/cdn/product-assets/product.bi.additional.info.pdf/31203_ES_BI_Info_Booklet.pdf). Blocks uses rectangular two-stud tiles rather than reproducing the reference's single-stud art pieces. Its TSL implementation is original; it follows the shared orientation-aware texture sampling contract and [Three's TSL texture API](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/TSL.html).

Stud spacing is measured in authored image pixels. Grid center and direction transform both brick geometry and its source lookups together. Optional alternating row offsets form a running bond. Five fixed source samples per brick are averaged in premultiplied space and divided by their summed alpha, preventing invisible source RGB from polluting brick color. An exact integer seed controls restrained per-brick color variation. The per-pixel original alpha is retained, with optional antialiased transparent cuts between bricks. Amount blends coverage in premultiplied space, so zero amount is the original image.

Plastic relief uses analytic screen-space geometry, not real meshes or a scene depth buffer. A normal field gives each stud a rounded cap, a sidewall and a cast shadow. Stud radius and height control the raised cap and its shadow. Optional per-channel color quantization reduces source colors to discrete levels; zero keeps the source palette. Relief zero retains flat sampled brick color; it does not remove the mosaic. Gap width is constrained to less than half a stud pitch so extreme legal settings retain visible bricks. Antialias width follows the pixel footprint, while geometry and seed remain independent of raster density. There are six bounded texture lookups including the original source, no extra render targets, no CPU pixel copies and no implicit animation clock.

Visual acceptance requires a photograph to retain recognizable large-scale colors while visibly resolving into two-stud brick tiles at useful spacing. Inspect opaque and graded-alpha inputs, zero gap, rotated/staggered grids, relief zero and full relief, high unsigned seeds, and partial amount. Test the shared WebGPU and WebGL2 backends, masked/nested output and decoded export at multiple zoom levels. Graph tests establish bounded dependencies and uniform identity only; they do not establish rendered stud quality.
